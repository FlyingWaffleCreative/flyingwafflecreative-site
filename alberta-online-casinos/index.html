Alberta's Regulated iGaming Market — What You Need to Know

Alberta became the second Canadian province — after Ontario — to open its iGaming market to licensed private operators. The framework is designed to channel players away from unregulated offshore sites toward licensed platforms with player protections, responsible gambling tools, and dispute resolution mechanisms.

🗓️ Key Timeline

  • 2020: PlayAlberta launches as the only regulated online casino in Alberta.
  • 2024: Bill 16 (Red Tape Reduction Act) updates the legal framework, clarifying provincial authority over online gaming.
  • 2025: Bill 48 — the iGaming Alberta Act — passes, establishing the multi-operator regulated market.
  • July 13, 2026: Alberta's regulated iGaming market goes live. BetMGM, DraftKings, bet365, Jackpot City, and others launch on Day 1.
  • October 13, 2026: Remaining operators in transition complete their rollout.

🏛️ Who Regulates Alberta Online Casinos?

Two agencies share oversight of the Alberta iGaming market:

  • Alberta Gaming, Liquor and Cannabis (AGLC): Issues licences, enforces compliance, and sets regulatory standards.
  • Alberta iGaming Corporation (AiGC): Manages day-to-day market operations. Check the AiGC's official directory to verify any operator's registration status.

⚖️ Quick Legal Facts

  • Legal age: 18+ (lower than Ontario's 19+)
  • Taxes on winnings: None for casual players — gambling winnings are not taxable income in Canada.
  • Offshore sites: Technically not illegal to use as a player, but they lack Alberta's consumer protections and dispute mechanisms.
  • Responsible gambling tools: All licensed sites must offer deposit limits, cool-off periods, and self-exclusion.

⚠️ Only Use Licensed Operators

Stick to AiGC-registered operators. Licensed casinos are legally required to pay out winnings, protect your personal data, and provide problem gambling support. Offshore sites have none of these obligations.

Alberta Gambling Glossary — Key Terms Defined

New to online gambling in Alberta? Here's what the key terms actually mean.

🎰

Online Casino

A licensed website or app offering digital versions of casino games — slots, blackjack, roulette, baccarat, poker, and live dealer tables. Players wager real money and can win real money. In Alberta, all legal online casinos must be registered with the AiGC.

⚽

Sportsbook

A platform where you place bets on the outcomes of real-world sporting events — hockey, football, basketball, soccer, UFC, and more. Odds are expressed as moneylines, point spreads, or totals. Many Alberta platforms (BetMGM, DraftKings, bet365) combine a sportsbook with an online casino under one account.

📈

Polymarket (Prediction Markets)

Polymarket is a global prediction market platform where users bet on the outcomes of real-world events — elections, economic indicators, sports results, and more — using cryptocurrency. Unlike regulated sportsbooks, Polymarket operates as a decentralized exchange. It is not currently licensed in Alberta and does not fall under AiGC jurisdiction. Canadian users should verify their eligibility before participating.

🎲

Live Dealer Casino

A casino game streamed in real time from a physical studio, with a real human dealer managing cards, dice, or a roulette wheel. You see the action via video stream and place bets digitally. It bridges the gap between online convenience and the atmosphere of a physical casino.

🎁

Welcome Bonus

An offer new players receive when depositing for the first time — usually a matched deposit bonus (e.g., 100% up to $500) or free spins. Always read the wagering requirements (how many times you must bet the bonus before withdrawing) before claiming.

💸

RTP (Return to Player)

The theoretical percentage of all wagered money a slot machine pays back over time. A slot with 96% RTP returns $96 for every $100 wagered over millions of spins. Higher RTP = better odds. Always check this before choosing a slot.
